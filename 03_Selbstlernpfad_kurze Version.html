  1. Read the power check below.

Power check
  • Can they make or block decisions (e.g. votes, laws)?
  • How much does LEGO depend on what they provide – can it be replaced easily?
  • Are they many, and do they act together (e.g. customers, trade unions)?

Useful language: “… has a high influence on the LEGO Group because …” · “… has only a low influence because …” · “It depends: if …, then …”

Did you know?

The LEGO Group is not listed on the stock exchange. It belongs to KIRKBI A/S, the holding company of the Kirk Kristiansen family (75 %), and the LEGO Foundation (25 %). So LEGO’s owners have a very strong influence.

Source: The LEGO Group, “Ownership”, lego.com/aboutus/lego-group/ownership, accessed 18 October 2026.

Goals of a company

The goal of every company should be to meet the interests of all its stakeholders. This is the only way a business can be successful in the long run. However, this is often easier said than done, because the interests of the different stakeholders can be in conflict with each other.

Step 5 · individually · practice 2 of 3

Complementary or conflicting?

  1. Decide for each pair of goals: do they support each other or are they in conflict?
  2. Read the explanation after each answer.
Remember

Complementary goals: reaching one goal also helps to achieve the other – a “win-win”.
Conflicting goals: working toward one goal makes it harder to reach the other.

💡 Help – if you get stuck

Ask: if the company reaches goal A – does goal B become easier (complementary) or harder (conflicting)?
